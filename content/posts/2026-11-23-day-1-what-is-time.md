I've been going down a rabbit hole about time lately, and it's already getting deeper than I expected. It started with looking into how physical clocks actually work. Whether it's a swinging pendulum or a vibrating quartz crystal, an oscillator just repeats a steady motion and the clock counts it. That part made sense, until a much harder question hit me: what is time, actually?

My first instinct was simple. It's the ticking of a clock, the seconds, minutes, hours, and years passing by. Wait, no. That's like saying inches and miles are space. Those are just units we invented to measure time; they aren't time itself. A clock can tell me how many cycles just happened, but it doesn't tell me what's actually moving underneath. So what exactly are we measuring when we say time passes?
