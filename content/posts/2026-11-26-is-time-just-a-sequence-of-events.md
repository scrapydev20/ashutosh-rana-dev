Last time I realized clocks just count units; they don't actually tell me what time is. So I started wondering if time is just a sequence of events.

First I had to figure out what an "event" even means. From what I've been reading, it's basically just a change of state. A glass breaks, or a bit flips from 0 to 1. Turns out, the philosopher Leibniz argued something similar centuries ago. He thought time isn't a real thing on its own, just the relationship between things changing. If literally nothing changed anywhere, time wouldn't exist at all. And the order obviously matters because of cause and effect. You have to drop the glass before it can shatter.

I thought I had a pretty solid answer there, until I looked into the other side of the debate. Newton saw it the opposite way. He pictured time more like an invisible container that keeps flowing even if the universe is completely empty. And honestly, calling time "just a sequence" does leave a big hole. Saying "A happened, then B" tells you the order, sure, but it says nothing about the gap in between. Was it a millisecond or a hundred years? Then Einstein's relativity makes it even weirder. If two events happen far enough apart that neither could've caused the other, people moving at different speeds can actually disagree on which one came first, and somehow they're both right.

So a sequence of events isn't the whole picture. If time isn't just a list of things happening one after another, is it an empty container like Newton thought, or is something else filling the gap between events?
